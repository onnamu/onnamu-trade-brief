1Overview and structure

On 8 December 2026tentative, CEO Seokmin Lee will meet a vice minister or top-level advisor of Kemendes PDT (Kementerian Desa dan Pembangunan Daerah Tertinggal; Ministry of Village and Development of Disadvantaged Regions). He is expected to receive export-candidate product lists from about 500 of some 1,100 TEKAD villages and take the first step toward becoming that ministry’s official agent.

Phase 1 of the November 2026 proposal is Indonesia → Korea brokerage: official agent appointment (LOA) in about 4–6 weeks; sharing verified sourcing information with KOTRA and KITA; a March–May 2027 trade expo (working title 2027 Korea-Indonesia Trade Expo: Indonesian Export Sourcing Fair, at COEX or KINTEX, or JCC in Jakarta if held locally); then contract, logistics and customs support, plus commission settlement.

Phase 2 (from H2 2027) would broker Korean technology, machinery and equipment, and K-consumer goods into Indonesian procurement and distribution once expo and ministry trust is in place. 5Market research highlights

Chicken feet — imports into Korea not currently possible

  • Indonesian poultry cannot currently be imported into Korea. Indonesia is not on the Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency (QIA) approved-country list.
  • Korea HS 0207.14-2090 basic tariff 27%. Mainly Brazilian and Thai origin.
  • Korean wholesale approx. KRW 6,600–6,900/kg. Brazilian origin approx. KRW 3,200/kg (2026-08-21). Local Indonesian prices approx. KRW 1,180–2,360/kg.

Opportunity: reverse export of halal Korean-style spicy chicken feet to Indonesia.

Coffee — green beans and halal ingredients

  • 29,101 coffee franchise outlets in Korea (end of 2024).
  • Korea’s 2025 coffee imports rose 35% in value to USD 1.86 billion; volume was roughly unchanged.
  • Indonesia’s coffee market: about USD 6.7 billion (2024), projected to reach USD 12.6 billion by 2030.
  • Green coffee production: 12.37 million bags (2025/26, USDA). Halal mandatory for medium and large foodservice since October 2024.

Opportunity: supply Indonesian green coffee and halal ingredients to Korea.

Team market research (2026-11-25). Tariff rates and the approved-country list require expert confirmation with QIA and the Korea Customs Service (KCS).

8Key risks and next steps

  • Securing buyers is the key. The roles document states that lining up genuine Korean end-buyer demand in advance is difficult under any model. There are now two importer acquaintances (seafood; general industrial goods) plus Woobae Kim, the first importer to be introduced (ONNAMU partner). The window must be broadened to government or public-agency level to invite multiple buyers with real purchasing power.
  • Chicken feet (poultry) blocked by quarantine. Indonesia is not QIA-approved, so these cannot currently enter Korea. A separately researched item, not one of the four candidate products. The opportunity is Korea → Indonesia halal spicy chicken feet.
  • Reconfirm prices and delivery terms. Prices of the four candidate products fluctuate. Frozen squid: delivery terms not stated. Fish bait: shipping excluded.
  • The brokerage entity needs to be aligned. The proposal has “our company” earning commissions as official agent; in models ② and ④ a KIIP affiliate brokers and Seokmin Lee does not. Align the entity before the 8 December meeting (company, ministry, and firm counts are still blank).
  • Next steps: Confirm meeting time and place; draft LOA items; after the ~500-product list arrives, set the platform kickoff date; reconfirm the four products’ prices and squid delivery terms; decide the brokerage entity (ONNAMU / a KIIP affiliate / a separate legal entity).
